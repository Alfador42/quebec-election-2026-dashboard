Pallas Quebec Poll: PQ 29, PLQ 28, CAQ 19, PCQ 14, QS 8

  • May 14, 2026
  • Pallas Data

(TORONTO, May 14, 2026) – The Parti québécois under Paul St-Pierre Plamondon has taken first place in Quebec provincial voting intentions, according to a new Pallas Data survey conducted for QC125.

Among decided and leaning voters, the PQ sits at 29%, with the Liberal Party of Quebec under Charles Milliard at 28%. The Coalition avenir Québec under Christine Fréchette stands at 19%, with the Parti conservateur du Québec under Éric Duhaime at 14% and Québec solidaire under Ruba Ghazal at 8%.

“The PQ has a narrow lead, but the bigger story is how compressed the top of this field has become,” said Dr. Joseph Angolano, Founder and CEO of Pallas Data. “Three weeks ago, the Liberals led. Now the PQ leads. The campaign hasn’t started, and the race is already this volatile.”

Voting Intentions

Among all voters, the PQ leads at 26%, followed by the PLQ at 25%, the CAQ at 17%, the PCQ at 14%, and QS at 7%. Ten percent remain undecided.

Language divides the race as sharply as ever. Among non-francophone voters, the PLQ commands 56% of the decided vote. Among francophones, the PQ leads with 35%, followed by the PLQ at 20%, with the CAQ at 22%.

On the Island of Montreal, the PLQ reaches 54% of decided voters. In the Quebec City RMR, the PQ leads at 34%, followed by the CAQ at 22% and the PCQ at 18%. In Montreal’s suburbs, the PCQ pulls 27%. The CAQ’s 19% overall is concentrated among older francophones and suburban voters outside the island.

QS support skews young. The party draws 13% among 18-to-34-year-olds in the decided electorate, against just 4% among voters 65 and older.

Government Performance

The Fréchette government earns mixed grades. Overall, 42% of respondents express some dissatisfaction with its performance, compared with 22% who are satisfied and 21% who are neutral. Fifteen percent say they don’t know.

The partisan contours are predictable: CAQ voters are the most satisfied (49% somewhat or very satisfied), QS voters the most opposed (58% very dissatisfied). The more notable number is in the PQ cohort: 37% of Plamondon’s voters can’t rate the government, which suggests a meaningful share of his support comes from people who are checked out of the current government rather than actively hostile to it.

Best Premier

On best premier, Plamondon leads at 25%, with Fréchette at 22%, Milliard at 20%, Duhaime at 11%, and Ghazal at 7%. Fifteen percent say none of the above or don’t know.

Among francophones, Plamondon leads with 32%, followed by Fréchette at 25% and Milliard at 14%. Fréchette running second overall on best premier is a structural asset for the CAQ: some voters who have drifted from the party still see her as the most credible leader in the field.

Milliard’s third-place finish despite the PLQ’s competitive vote share is a problem. His support is concentrated among non-francophone voters (36%) and PLQ loyalists. Outside that coalition, he isn’t pulling.

Sovereignty

Just under six in ten Quebec voters (59%) say they would vote against sovereignty in a referendum, with 31% in favour and 10% undecided.

The generational pattern is sharp. Voters aged 50 to 64 are the most opposed at 67%. Among 18-to-34-year-olds, 48% would vote No, and 43% would vote Yes. The rest of Quebec, outside the major urban centres, is the most sovereigntist region, at 37% in favour.

Partisan dynamics tell the story of the sovereigntist coalition’s limits. Among PQ voters, 78% would vote Yes. Among QS voters, only 35% would. Among CAQ voters, 18%, and among PCQ voters, 17%.

“The Yes side needs to reach beyond committed PQ voters to build a winning coalition, and that hasn’t happened,” said Angolano. “Younger voters are more open to sovereignty than their elders, but ‘more open’ and ‘committed’ are not the same thing. The 18-to-34 gap is real, but it’s soft.”

Party Leaders

Duhaime has the worst net favourables of any leader tested: 57% combined unfavourable. His 16% overall favourability is almost entirely a function of PCQ loyalty. Among QS voters, his unfavourable rating reaches 93%.

Plamondon is the most polarizing of the sovereigntist leaders. His combined unfavourable rating is 48%, driven by PLQ voters (78% unfavourable) and QS voters (80% unfavourable). His 34% combined favourable is concentrated in his own coalition.

Fréchette and Milliard occupy a similar middle ground. Fréchette earns 31% favourable overall, with strength among older voters and CAQ loyalists. Milliard’s 22% overall rises to 33% among non-francophone Quebecers.

Ghazal has the lowest combined favourable in the field at 20%. Her 46% combined unfavourable rating suggests most non-QS voters have already formed a negative view of her. Among QS supporters, 84% view her favourably.

Methodology

Pallas Data conducted this survey on May 7 and 9, 2026, using a random sample of 1,176 adults aged 18 and over living in the province of Quebec. The survey was conducted via interactive voice response (IVR), reaching respondents on both landlines and mobile phones. Responses were weighted using demographic information from the 2021 Census to be representative of Quebec’s adult population. This survey was commissioned by QC125. The margin of error is ±2.9 percentage points at a 95% confidence level. Margins of error are larger for subsamples. Totals may not add to 100% due to rounding.
